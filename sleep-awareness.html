Sleep & Insomnia Awareness

Learn what persistent sleep difficulties can mean, explore practical steps, and understand when to speak with a healthcare professional.

Not a diagnosis: This educational guide is not a validated sleep test. It does not calculate a clinical score or determine whether you have insomnia.

Which sleep challenges are you noticing?

Falling asleep

It frequently takes a long time to fall asleep.

Staying asleep

You wake up repeatedly or earlier than intended.

Daytime effects

Tiredness, difficulty concentrating or irritability affects daily life.

Ongoing problems

Sleep problems persist or recur despite opportunities to rest.

These are examples for self-reflection only—not test items or a score.

Practical things to try

  • Keep a fairly consistent time to wake up, including weekends.
  • Create a quiet, comfortable sleep environment.
  • Notice whether caffeine, late meals or screen use affect your sleep.
  • If sleep difficulties persist, ask a clinician about evidence-based treatment such as cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I).

When to seek help

Speak with a qualified healthcare professional when sleep difficulties persist, affect daytime activities or occur with loud snoring, breathing pauses, severe sleepiness or other concerning symptoms. Avoid driving if dangerously sleepy.

Validated assessment options under review

We are reviewing the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) and Sleep Condition Indicator (SCI). These are established assessment tools, but MindCheck will not reproduce or score them without confirming appropriate usage permissions and clinical review.
