Suicide Prevention & Support

Support is available. You do not have to complete a questionnaire to ask for urgent help.

If you or someone else may be in immediate danger: call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department now. If possible, stay with a trusted person and move away from anything you could use to harm yourself. Do not wait for a website response.

If you are worried about yourself

  • Tell a trusted person what is happening and ask them to stay with you or help you reach care.
  • Contact a qualified healthcare professional, crisis service or nearby hospital promptly.
  • If speaking feels hard, consider showing a trusted person this page or sending a short message asking for immediate support.

If you are worried about someone else

  • Listen without judgment and take concerns seriously.
  • Ask directly whether they are thinking about suicide; do not promise secrecy if immediate safety is at stake.
  • Help them connect with professional care. If danger is immediate, seek emergency assistance and stay with them when it is safe to do so.

What about a suicide-risk questionnaire?

Tools such as the Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) support structured assessment, but a questionnaire cannot guarantee safety or replace a trained response. MindCheck is reviewing clinical use with its healthcare partners before offering any scored suicide-risk tool.
