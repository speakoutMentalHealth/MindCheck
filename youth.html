Youth wellbeing

Start with something small

Give feelings a name

Feelings can change. You might feel happy, worried, sad or excited. All feelings deserve attention.

Take a gentle pause

Try breathing comfortably: breathe in gently, then breathe out slowly. Stop if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable.

Find someone to talk to

You can tell a trusted adult, teacher or healthcare professional what is on your mind.

Care for your day

Rest, movement, time outdoors and staying connected can support wellbeing.

EVERYDAY WELLBEING

Little skills for everyday life

Choose a topic to try. These are general learning activities, not tests. Nothing you choose here is recorded or sent anywhere.

When school feels stressful

Pick one small task, take a short break, and ask a teacher or trusted adult for help if the work feels overwhelming.

Friendship and boundaries

It is okay to say no, ask for space, and tell a trusted adult if someone makes you feel unsafe or pressured.

Big feelings

Notice what you are feeling. Try naming it, finding a quiet place, or talking with someone you trust. You do not need to solve everything at once.

Online wellbeing

Take breaks from screens, protect your personal information, and speak to a trusted adult about bullying or upsetting messages.

When you need extra help

If a feeling is too much to handle alone, talk to a trusted adult or a qualified healthcare professional. If you feel unsafe, tell a trusted adult right away. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.
